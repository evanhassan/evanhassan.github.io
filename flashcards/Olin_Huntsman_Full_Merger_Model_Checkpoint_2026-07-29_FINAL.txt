5. SHARE / EQUITY RABBIT HOLE — FINAL CONCLUSION
======================================================================

Rejected default approach:
- Net Shares Issued / (Repurchased)
  x an implied market price
  = Stock Issuance / (Repurchase) cash flow

Why it was rejected:
- net share movement does not identify whether activity was cash issuance, SBC, treasury reissuance, retirement, or offsetting gross issuance and repurchases;
- an EBITDA / net-debt implied price creates fake precision;
- using same-year net debt can create a circularity through ending cash;
- it can turn noncash SBC dilution into fake financing proceeds.

Locked template principle:
- share-count activity and cash stock activity are separate concepts;
- do not infer exact cash from net shares alone;
- if the template later needs an exact $50mm buyback, it needs a separate cash-dollar assumption or an explicit capital-allocation rule.

Equity accounting:
- Common Stock records par value, not market value.
- New issuance:
  - Common Stock increases by new shares x par value.
  - APIC receives the residual equity credit / proceeds.
- Treasury-stock buyback:
  - cash decreases by repurchase cost;
  - Treasury Stock becomes more negative by repurchase cost;
  - Common Stock and APIC generally do not change.
- Retirement:
  - Common Stock decreases by par value;
  - the remaining cost reduces APIC and potentially retained earnings.
- Exact treasury-share reissuance accounting requires historical treasury cost basis.

Standalone base-case convention:
- do not fabricate forecast cash issuance proceeds merely because forecast shares increase;
- SBC remains a separate noncash expense / APIC driver.

- APIC had accidentally linked to Dividends Paid after a row shift.
- The correct stock-activity row was restored.
